Current Funds

What you actually have left to spend, and how it has moved over time.

  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} The figure is income − expenses − money moved into savings. It is worked out from your transactions every time the page loads, so it is never a stale number.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} The chart plots your balance day by day. The dashed line is a trend fitted through it — a quick read on whether you are drifting up or down over the period, rather than reacting to one big week.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Moving money to a savings fund lowers this figure but not your net worth: it is a transfer, not spending, so it stays out of your expense totals.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Use Add Income and Add Expense below to change it, and Transactions to see or correct anything already recorded.

Emergency Fund

Your savings, and whether they would actually carry you through a bad month.

  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} The target is three months of the expenses you marked essential. Mark them on the Expected Monthly Expenses tab — the target sizes itself from your real bills rather than a round number.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Covers is how many months of essentials the balance would pay for. Will last is different: it projects from the rate you have actually been taking money out.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Add moves cash in, Take out moves it back. Neither counts as income or spending, so your monthly figures stay honest.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} You can keep several funds — a holiday, a car — each with its own target and a projection of when you will reach it at your current rate.

Monthly Income

What you earn, where it comes from, and what to expect next month.

  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} The doughnut groups income by the source you type in, so keep the wording consistent — "Salary" every month rather than "salary" then "pay".
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Once there are three months of history you get an expected range rather than one average, because a single figure is misleading if your income varies.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Adding income automatically funds your recurring expenses, top of the priority list first. Check the result on the Expected Monthly Expenses tab.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Record one with Add Income below.

Monthly Expenses

What you spent, on what, and whether your bills are covered.

  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} The doughnut groups spending by the category you enter — add $500 of Food and Food becomes a slice. Split a shop trip into items and each one counts under its own category.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} The essential / non-essential split under the total is the needs-versus-wants view, and it is what sizes your emergency fund target.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Open Recurring monthly expenses to list bills like rent and rank them. Income fills that list from the top down, so you find out you are short before a payment is due — and which bill is at risk.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} A bill that varies, like electricity, learns its own cost from the payments you record against it. Link one on the Add Expense form.
