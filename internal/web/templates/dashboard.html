Current Funds

The money you actually have left to spend right now, and how it's changed over time.

  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} This number is everything you've earned, minus what you've spent, minus what you've moved into savings. It's worked out fresh every time you open the page, so it's always up to date.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} The chart plots your balance day by day. The sentence under it says whether you're generally trending up or down, instead of reacting to one unusual day.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Moving money into a savings fund isn't "spending" — it's just moved to a different pot. So sending $100 to your Emergency Fund lowers this number, but it doesn't hurt your net worth or count as an expense.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Use Add Income and Add Expense below to record something new, and Transactions to see everything you've entered — or fix a mistake.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Your savings funds — a holiday, a car — are listed below the chart. Create one with New savings fund, then use Add and Take out to move money in and out. The emergency fund has its own tab.

Emergency Fund

Money set aside to get you through a rough month, like a lost job or an unexpected bill.

  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} The target is set automatically to 3 months of the bills you've marked essential (on the Monthly Expenses tab) — so it's based on your real bills, not a guess.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Covers is how many months of essential bills your current balance could pay for today. Will last is different — it looks at how fast you've actually been withdrawing and estimates when the fund would run dry at that pace.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Add puts money in, Take out takes it back out. Neither counts as income or an expense — it's just money moving between pots, so your monthly totals stay accurate.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} This tab shows only your emergency fund. Other savings funds — a holiday, a car — live on the Current Funds tab, each with its own target and its own estimate of when you'll reach it at your current pace.

Monthly Income

What you've earned, where it came from, and roughly what to expect next.

  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} The chart groups income by whatever you type into Source — so use the same wording every time (always "Salary", not "salary" one month and "pay" the next), or the chart will split it into separate slices by mistake.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Once you have 3 months of history, you get an expected range instead of one average — a single number can be misleading if your income changes month to month.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Adding income here also automatically helps pay off your recurring bills, starting with whichever one you ranked highest. Check the result on the Monthly Expenses tab.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Recurring income, below the chart, lists every paycheck or other income YABA records for you automatically. Edit or cancel one here or on Add Income.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Record a new payment with Add Income below.

Monthly Expenses

What you've spent, broken down by category, and whether your bills are covered.

  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} The chart groups spending by whatever category you choose — like "Food" or "Rent". If one shopping trip covers several categories, split it into items so each part counts correctly.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Essential vs. non-essential just means needs versus wants. It matters because your essential spending is what sets your Emergency Fund target.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} Open Recurring expenses to list monthly bills (like rent) and rank them by importance. As income comes in, it pays off the list from the top down — so you find out ahead of time if you're short, and exactly which bill is at risk. The same section also lists the expenses YABA records for you automatically, and appears under Add Expense → Manual too.
  • {{template "icon" "check-circle"}} A bill that changes every month, like electricity, can learn its typical cost from the payments you record against it — just link it when adding the expense.
